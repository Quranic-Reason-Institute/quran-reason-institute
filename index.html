Why this institute exists

Fourteen centuries of drift.

The Qur'an tells us what happened to the people of Moses when he was gone for forty days. They returned to idol worship. That account is recorded as a warning about how quickly a community loses the thread — not as a piece of ancient history you are meant to feel superior to.

Now hold that number against fourteen hundred years. Political dynasties with an interest in obedience. A clerical class with an income. Narrations compiled two centuries after the Prophet died. Rituals borrowed from Zoroastrian practice and given Arabic names. Scholars treated as lords. And the book itself, gradually, set aside.

This institute began because we read the Qur'an and could not find most of what we had been told was obligatory. So we studied the Arabic. We traced the root words. We followed the evidence where it went, and we read the researchers who had gone there before us — Dr Sayed Abdul Wadud, Aurangzaib Yousufzai, Siraj Islam, Joseph Islam.

The Qur'an describes itself as complete, fully detailed and sufficient. We did not leave anything out of the Book. It was the first thing abandoned.

Drawing on Dr Sayed Abdul Wadud, Conspiracies Against the Qur'an (1976) and Quranocracy (1990); and the work of Aurangzaib Yousufzai, Siraj Islam and Joseph Islam.

The historical record

How the message was replaced.

This is not a theory about hidden hands. It is a documented sequence, and the Qur'an warned that it would happen.

  1. 1Year 40 AH

    The Umayyad dynasty seizes control.

    The old Meccan nobility — who resisted the message until the last moment — were now administering it. Their problem: a book demanding justice and equality threatened their power. Their solution was not to ban it. It was to redefine the religion around it so that it could no longer threaten anyone.

    Qur'an 6:159
  2. 250–100 AH

    Namaaz is introduced as the mandatory prayer.

    The command to establish salat — a whole system of conduct — was narrowed into a five-minute physical routine modelled on Zoroastrian practice. The effect was precise: people feel observant, and nothing in the world is required to change.

    Qur'an 107:4–7
  3. 3100–200 AH

    Hadith is compiled at scale, against explicit prohibition.

    All four of the first Caliphs had forbidden the writing of narrations. Two centuries later Bukhari gathered some 600,000 reports and discarded roughly 98.8% of them as unreliable. The surviving fraction justified the new rituals, elevated the Prophet beyond anything the Qur'an claims for him, and handed clerics the authority to legislate.

    Qur'an 77:50
  4. 4200–300 AH

    The schools of Fiqh close the door on reasoning.

    The four major schools fixed their rulings as permanently binding and declared the gates of ijtihad shut. From that point the Qur'an was memorised, recited and kissed — and no longer consulted as the source of law.

    Qur'an 9:31
  5. 5300+ AH

    Sufism, shrine devotion and sectarianism complete the picture.

    Mysticism drawn from Greek and Hindu sources replaced the call to reform the world with private inner experience. Today hundreds of millions perform namaaz faithfully while the societies around them fail on exactly the terms the Qur'an set out.

    Qur'an 25:30
And the Messenger will say: my Lord, my people have abandoned this Qur'an. Qur'an 25:30

Six ways the message was corrupted.

Each mechanism is documented, and each one is anticipated somewhere in the text it displaced.

God replaced with human intermediaries

The direct relationship between a person and their Creator was broken and refilled with functionaries. Priests became necessary. Imams and saints became the authorities people actually submitted to. The Qur'an is unambiguous that nothing stands between you and God.

They have taken their scholars and priests as lords besides God.Qur'an 9:31

The community split into sects

Political weakening through manufactured division, ideological corruption through fabricated narration, and cultural absorption from Persian, Jewish and Zoroastrian sources. The result is hundreds of sects, each certain it holds the exclusive truth.

Those who divided their religion into sects — you are not of them in anything.Qur'an 6:159

The text itself was attacked

Sectarian groups claimed either that the Qur'an is corrupted, or that it carries hidden meanings accessible only through their own authorities. Both claims serve the same purpose: they make the book unusable without a middleman.

Indeed, We have sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it.Qur'an 15:9

Action was replaced with passivity

Sufism entered wearing Islamic vocabulary, but its roots are in Greek philosophy and Hindu mysticism. It substituted inner experience and shrine devotion for the Qur'an's demand that the world be reformed — neutralising social reform without ever contradicting it openly.

Do you order people to righteousness while you forget yourselves?Qur'an 2:44

Salat was replaced with namaaz

A comprehensive discipline of conduct became a physical routine performed five times a day. It is the single most consequential substitution in the whole history, because it converts the central obligation of the religion into something that costs nothing and changes nothing.

Establish salat. Salat prevents indecency and wrongdoing.Qur'an 29:45

God's sovereignty was replaced with man's

The Umayyads imported the divine right of kings and dressed it in Qur'anic language. Dr Wadud's Quranocracy argues that democracy, autocracy and theocracy share one flaw: each places final authority in human hands.

The command is for none but God.Qur'an 12:40

Our central finding

Salat is not ritual prayer.

The most consequential mistranslation in the religion. Here is exactly how we know.

ص ل و

The root of salat is S-L-W. In Lane's Lexicon — the standard reference for classical Arabic — it carries the sense of following closely, pursuing, remaining attached, communicating, connecting.

In classical Arabic horse racing, the horse running so close behind the leader that its head overlaps the leader's flank is called al-mussalli: the one performing salat. The word describes sustained close following. It does not describe a posture.

The grammatical proof

In 2:45 and 2:153 the Qur'an pairs salat with sabr — patience — under a single governing verb. In Arabic, when one verb governs two nouns, both must be of the same type. Sabr is unambiguously abstract. Salat therefore cannot be a physical ritual. This is a point of grammar, not a matter of opinion.

We examined all 102 occurrences of salat in the Qur'an. Not one specifies a posture, a daily timing, or an Arabic phrase to recite. Every occurrence is consistent with the root: connection, communication, close following, the reform of conduct.

Why salat is not ritual prayer

All salat studies

  • Salat prevents wrongdoing

    The Qur'an states that salat prevents indecency and wrongdoing. A five-minute physical exercise demonstrably does not prevent wrongdoing; the societies that perform it most faithfully are not the most just. A comprehensive discipline of living does.

    Qur'an 29:45

  • Birds perform salat

    The verse says birds perform salat. Birds do not bow, prostrate or recite Arabic formulas. They do follow closely the patterns built into creation — which is precisely what the root means.

    Qur'an 24:41

  • Salat during active battle

    The passage describes salat performed in shifts during combat, weapons in hand, one group standing guard. As a description of ritual prayer under fire it is strained. As a description of a briefing and reorganisation under threat it is exact.

    Qur'an 4:101–103

  • A lapsed salat is restored by good works

    Those who let salat lapse are told to restore it by doing good works — not by performing more prayers. That instruction only makes sense if salat was never a prayer count in the first place.

    Qur'an 19:59–60

A key to reading the book

The stories are parables.

The Qur'an uses the word mathal — parable, analogy — of its own narratives, and says plainly that they require knowledge to understand.

Noah is not primarily a man who lived a long time and built a boat. He is the archetype of the truth-seeker in a society that will not hear him. The Ark is guidance itself. The flood is worldly appetite and shallow thinking closing over everything.

Once you see this, the whole book reads differently. Abraham, Moses, Jesus, the bird imagery, the descriptions of the garden and the fire — these are structures carrying principles about human nature and divine guidance, and they were understood that way long before anyone insisted on reading them as reportage.

My point is not that those ancient people told literal stories and we are now smart enough to take them symbolically — but that they told them symbolically and we are now dumb enough to take them literally.John Dominic Crossan, biblical scholar

Refutations

Fifteen arguments for hadith. Fifteen answers.

Every standard argument for hadith authority, answered from the Qur'an's own text. Bukhari was born 194 years after the Prophet died. The instruction in 17:36 is: do not follow what you have no knowledge of.

01 “Obeying the Messenger means following hadith”

The Qur'an defines obedience to the Messenger as obedience to the revelation he delivered. It cannot mean books compiled two centuries after his death, which he neither wrote nor saw. He is quoted in the text itself: I follow only what is revealed to me.

Qur'an 24:54 · 6:50 · 5:99
02 “The Messenger explained the Qur'an, and hadith preserves his explanations”

The Qur'an says its verses are perfected and that it clarifies itself. The word in 16:44 is li-tubayyina — to make evident, to deliver plainly. It does not mean to append a second body of content. He delivered the book; he did not produce a supplement to it.

Qur'an 16:44 · 25:33 · 11:1
03 “Hadith supplies the details of salat that the Qur'an omits”

We did not leave anything out of the Book. The perceived gap exists only if you have already assumed salat means namaaz. Read the word at its root and the instruction is complete as given.

Qur'an 6:38 · 29:45 · 16:89
04 “The Qur'an is too general; hadith fills in specifics”

The Qur'an calls itself mufassal — fully detailed — and asks directly whether it is not sufficient that the Book was sent down. If God calls it sufficient, arguing that it requires supplementing is a disagreement with God, not with us.

Qur'an 29:51 · 41:3 · 6:114
05 “God preserved hadith as part of preserving the religion”

God promises to preserve exactly one thing, and names it: the Reminder. There is no equivalent promise anywhere in the text for any collection of narrations. The absence is not an oversight.

Qur'an 15:9
06 “The Prophet held legal authority beyond the Qur'an”

He states he follows only what is revealed to him. He is instructed to judge by what God sent down, and warned against following inclination or inherited practice in his judgments. His authority was the revelation. There was no second source.

Qur'an 6:50 · 5:48 · 5:49
07 “The companions followed hadith, so we should”

They followed their forefathers even when those forefathers understood nothing. And: if you obey most of those on Earth they will mislead you. Historical precedent is not an argument the Qur'an accepts.

Qur'an 2:170 · 6:116
08 “Rejecting hadith means rejecting the Prophet”

The Messenger's duty is only to convey clearly. Verse 59:7, endlessly cited here, concerns the distribution of war spoils in a specific situation. It is not a general grant of legislative authority to later compilers.

Qur'an 5:99 · 59:7
09 “Isnad science establishes which reports are authentic”

A chain of named narrators across two hundred years is human assessment of human memory. Bukhari himself discarded roughly 98.8% of what he collected — which is men grading men, not preservation by God. Do not follow what you have no knowledge of.

Qur'an 17:36 · 53:28
10 “Hadith fills the gaps the Qur'an left open”

We did not leave anything out of the Book. Today I have completed your system for you. You cannot affirm 5:3 and simultaneously maintain that the system was left incomplete.

Qur'an 6:38 · 5:3
11 “Sunnah is a second form of revelation”

53:3–4 refers to the Qur'an being conveyed, not to personal sayings. And if sunnah were revelation, it would have been preserved as revelation was preserved. It was not.

Qur'an 53:3–4 · 6:50 · 15:9
12 “The Prophet's judgments prove law exists outside the Qur'an”

He is commanded to judge between people by what God has sent down, and warned in the next verse not to follow their inclinations away from the truth that has come to him. The instruction is explicit and it is narrow.

Qur'an 5:48 · 5:49
13 “Scholarly consensus confirms hadith authority”

If you follow most of those on Earth they will mislead you from God's path. And: they have taken their scholars and priests as lords besides God. The Qur'an names this exact reasoning and warns against it.

Qur'an 6:116 · 9:31
14 “The same people preserved both the Qur'an and hadith”

God preserves the Reminder. Human transmitters preserved what human transmitters could. The Qur'an nowhere treats sincerity of transmission as equivalent to divine preservation, and the two must not be merged by assertion.

Qur'an 15:9
15 “You cannot practise Islam without hadith”

This Qur'an guides to that which is most upright. We have sent the Book as a clarification for all things. Hold fast to what We have revealed to you. The text describes itself as practical, complete and sufficient — three times over.

Qur'an 17:9 · 16:89 · 43:43
