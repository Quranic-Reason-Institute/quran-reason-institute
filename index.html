What we actually found

Salat is not ritual prayer.

One of our most significant findings. The word salat has been completely mistranslated. Here is exactly how we know.

First — the Arabic root

The Arabic root of Salat is ص-ل-و (S-L-W). According to Lane's Lexicon — the definitive classical Arabic dictionary — this root means: to follow closely, to pursue, to remain attached, to communicate, to connect.

In classical Arabic horse racing, the horse that follows so closely behind the leader that its head overlaps the first horse's flank is called Al-Mussalli — the one performing salat. The word describes close connection and following. Not ritual prayer.

The grammatical proof: The Qur'an pairs salat with sabr (patience) using one verb in verses like 2:45 and 2:153. In Arabic grammar, one verb governing two nouns means both must be the same type. Sabr is clearly abstract. Therefore salat cannot be a physical ritual. This is grammar, not opinion.

We studied all 102 occurrences of salat in the Qur'an. Not one specifies physical postures, daily timings, or Arabic phrases to recite. Every single use is consistent with the root meaning: connection, communication, following closely, reforming conduct.

Evidence 1. Salat prevents wrongdoing.

The Qur'an says salat prevents indecency and wrongdoing (29:45). A five-minute physical exercise does not prevent wrongdoing. A comprehensive system of living according to divine guidance does.

Qur'an 29:45

Evidence 2. Birds perform salat.

Qur'an 24:41 says birds perform salat. Birds do not bow and prostrate. But birds participate in divine patterns built into creation — which is exactly what salat means.

Qur'an 24:41

Evidence 3. Wartime salat.

Qur'an 4:101–103 describes salat during active battle — weapons ready, in shifts, standing guard. As a ritual prayer this makes no sense. As a cautionary briefing session it makes complete sense.

Qur'an 4:101–103

Evidence 4. Missed salat restored by good works.

Qur'an 19:59–60 says those who let salat lapse restore it through doing good works — not more prayer. This only makes sense if salat means righteous conduct, not a prayer schedule.

Qur'an 19:59–60

Hadith refutations

15 arguments for Hadith. 15 answers.

Every standard argument for Hadith authority, answered from the Qur'an's own text. Bukhari was born 194 years after the Prophet. The Qur'an says: "Do not follow what you have no knowledge of." (17:36)

01
"Obeying the Messenger means following Hadith"
The Qur'an defines obeying the Messenger as obeying the revelation he delivered — not books compiled 200 years after his death. The Prophet said: "I do not follow except what is revealed to me." (6:50)
Qur'an 24:54 · 6:50 · 5:99
02
"The Messenger explained the Qur'an — Hadith has his explanations"
The Qur'an says its verses are perfected (11:1) and that it clarifies itself (25:33). The word in 16:44 is li-tubayyina — to make evident, not to add external content. The Prophet delivered the Qur'an. He did not produce a supplementary book.
Qur'an 16:44 · 25:33 · 11:1
03
"Hadith gives the details of Salat the Qur'an does not provide"
"We did not leave anything out of the Book." (6:38) When you understand Salat through its actual Arabic root — not 300-year-old ritual assumptions — the Qur'an is entirely sufficient. "This Book is a clarification for all things." (16:89)
Qur'an 6:38 · 29:45 · 16:89
04
"The Qur'an is too general — Hadith fills in the specifics"
The Qur'an calls itself mufassal — fully detailed (41:3). God asks directly: "Is it not sufficient for them that We sent down the Book?" (29:51). If God says it is sufficient, claiming it needs supplementing contradicts His own word.
Qur'an 29:51 · 41:3 · 6:114
05
"God preserved Hadith as part of preserving the religion"
God promises to preserve exactly one thing: "Indeed, We have sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it." (15:9) That is the Qur'an. No such promise exists for any Hadith collection.
Qur'an 15:9
06
"The Prophet had legal authority beyond the Qur'an"
The Prophet said: "I do not follow except what is revealed to me." (6:50) He judged only by revelation (5:48). The Qur'an warns him against following tradition or personal desire in judgments (5:49). His authority was the Qur'an. Nothing else.
Qur'an 6:50 · 5:48 · 5:49
07
"The companions followed Hadith — so should we"
"They followed their forefathers even if they understood nothing." (2:170) "If you obey most of those on Earth, they will mislead you from God's path." (6:116) Truth is not determined by historical precedent.
Qur'an 2:170 · 6:116
08
"Rejecting Hadith means rejecting the Prophet"
"The Messenger's duty is only to convey clearly." (5:99) Verse 59:7 — constantly misused for this argument — addresses the distribution of war spoils in a specific historical situation, not a general mandate for Hadith as religious law.
Qur'an 5:99 · 59:7
09
"Isnad science verifies which Hadith are authentic"
Chains of oral narrators across 200 years are human conjecture, not divine preservation. "Do not follow what you have no knowledge of." (17:36) Bukhari rejected 98.8% of hadith he collected. That is men evaluating men — not divine preservation.
Qur'an 17:36 · 53:28
10
"Hadith fills the gaps the Qur'an left open"
"We did not leave anything out of the Book." (6:38) "Today I have completed your system for you." (5:3) God declares it complete. You cannot believe 5:3 and simultaneously claim the Qur'an is insufficient.
Qur'an 6:38 · 5:3
11
"Sunnah is a second form of divine revelation"
53:3–4 refers to the Qur'an being recited, not personal sayings. If Sunnah were divine revelation, God would have preserved it the way He preserved the Qur'an (15:9). He did not. That absence is the evidence.
Qur'an 53:3–4 · 6:50 · 15:9
12
"The Prophet's legal judgments prove law exists outside the Qur'an"
"Judge between them by what God has sent down." (5:48) "Do not follow their desires over the truth that has come to you." (5:49) The Qur'an commands the Prophet to judge only by revelation.
Qur'an 5:48 · 5:49
13
"Scholarly consensus confirms Hadith authority"
"If you follow most of those on Earth, they will mislead you from God's path." (6:116) "They have taken their scholars and priests as lords besides God." (9:31) The Qur'an directly warns against exactly this reasoning.
Qur'an 6:116 · 9:31
14
"The same people preserved both the Qur'an and Hadith"
"Indeed, We have sent down the Reminder, and We will surely preserve it." (15:9) God preserves the Qur'an, not human transmitters. The Qur'an never equates human sincerity with divine preservation.
Qur'an 15:9
15
"You cannot practice Islam without Hadith"
"This Qur'an guides to that which is most upright." (17:9) "We have sent the Book as clarification for all things." (16:89) "Hold fast to what We have revealed to you." (43:43) The Qur'an declares itself practical, complete, and sufficient.
Qur'an 17:9 · 16:89 · 43:43
